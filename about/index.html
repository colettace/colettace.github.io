About

Chris Coletta’s path from mechanical engineering to biomedical research and public-sector data engineering.

Baltimore, Maryland

I’m Chris Coletta, a data scientist based in Baltimore, Maryland. I work across data architecture, engineering, and analysis, with a particular interest in how complex measurements become reliable evidence.

From physical models to data systems

I began in mechanical engineering at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, where my research involved finite-element modeling of muscle forces on skeletal structures. Commercial software development at Vistagy then gave me experience building tools for aerospace manufacturing.

In 2009, I joined the National Institutes of Health in Baltimore as a computer scientist. Over the next thirteen years, I worked on biomedical image analysis, machine learning, and longitudinal aging research. I led development of WND-CHARM, an open-source biomedical image-classification toolkit, and contributed to collaborative research across several areas of aging biology.

Today, I work at Fearless as a Consultant in Data & Analytics, helping public-sector teams design and build data platforms. Through my separate business, Baltimore Quantitative, I also support scientific data integration and research workflows.

How I work

I like to understand a system from both ends: what the data means to the people collecting it, and what the people using it need to know. That means treating quality checks, metadata, documentation, and maintainability as part of the work from the beginning.

Teaching has also shaped my approach. I developed and taught introductory Python programming for bioinformatics students through FAES at NIH, and I mentor engineers through pair programming and technical coaching.
